Visualize Fluid Flow Through an Axisymmetric de Laval Nozzle

This rocket nozzle is a converging–diverging (de Laval) nozzle. High-enthalpy gas (high temperature, high pressure) from the combustion chamber speeds up through the converging section. If the pressure ratio is high enough, the flow goes sonic at the throat and keeps accelerating to supersonic speed in the diverging section, turning enthalpy into velocity and increasing thrust.

This visualization shows dots moving that are meant to be representative of the fluid moving. Each dot travels at the local speed U(x) from a quasi-1D calculation, and its color corresponds to the fluid temperature (red = hot, blue = cold). The nozzle is axisymmetric, so this is a side view: each dot stays at a fixed fraction of the local radius. A real nozzle would not be perfectly quasi-1D and the shock, if there is one, would not be perfectly planar. Change the conditions and watch the shock appear, move, and leave the nozzle.
