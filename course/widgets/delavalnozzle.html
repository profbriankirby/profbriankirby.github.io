Flow Through a de Laval Nozzle

A converging–diverging (de Laval) nozzle turns the pressure and temperature of a nearly stagnant reservoir into a high-speed jet. Treating the flow as quasi-1D and isentropic except across normal shocks, the flow pattern is set by the stagnation conditions p_0,\ T_0, the gas (R,\ \gamma), and the ambient (back) pressure p_\text{amb}.

If p_\text{amb} is close to p_0, the flow is subsonic everywhere. Lower it and the throat chokes (M=1); a normal shock then sits in the diverging section wherever it makes the exit pressure match p_\text{amb} (found here by bisection). Lower still and the shock leaves the nozzle, so the flow inside is isentropic and supersonic all the way to the exit.

Choose the conditions

Pressure sliders are logarithmic. The nozzle is 2D, 0.1 m deep, with exit-to-throat area ratio 3.75. Thrust is \dot m U_e + (p_e-p_\text{amb})A_e.
